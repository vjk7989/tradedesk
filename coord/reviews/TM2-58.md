Verdict: APPROVE (no blocker, no high). Diff and logic review only; I did not re-run the tests.

Scope: only `trade-mcp/test/tm3_holidays.edge.test.ts` (that test and its helpers) and `coord/reports/TM2-58.md` changed. No src. No skip. The functional asserts are kept (5000 parsed, NSE open, added 2026-01-26 closes it).

## Findings
- INFO test:237-238 - Windows safety holds. `minSampleMsFor(process.platform, hasThreadCpu)` reads the real platform (no stub). It returns 200 on win32 or on the cpuUsage fallback, and 60 only for non-win32 with threadCpuUsage. The mapping is asserted for win32/linux/darwin x threadCpu yes/no, so the 200 ms floor is pinned by code path.
- INFO test:240,267 - The 200 ms path still assumes the 15.6 ms tick (`tickMsFor(minMs>=200)`). A reading of <= 2 ticks grows k x4 and is never divided by a floor. Every accepted sample is >= 200 ms, so a near-zero win32 reading cannot enter the median (quantisation < 8%). The win32 path was exercised on Linux via a simulated floor (all samples >= 221 ms, passed).
- INFO test:237 - darwin: Node's threadCpuUsage uses thread_info(THREAD_BASIC_INFO), which has microsecond resolution, and Linux uses CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID. A 60 ms floor is fine on both. Unknown or older runtimes without threadCpuUsage fall to 200 ms, which is conservative.
- LOW test:233-234 - Ratio headroom: worst loaded checks ratio is 21.1x against 30x (1.42x), parse 17.4x, lists 14.0x. Median of 7 with 60 ms samples is acceptable: 20/20 loaded runs passed and the spread was tight. It is thinner than the parse and lists sides, and GC noise matters more on 60 ms samples than on 200 ms ones. Nobody needs to act unless it flakes; if it does, raise REPS or the floor before raising MAX_RATIO.
- LOW test:341-351 - Coverage change: the call-count ratio now uses the 250-date list instead of 5000. This is a reasonable trade: the 5000-date scan used to dilute a per-call growth term, and the quadratic proof now reaches about 164x versus about 100x. Parse (list length at load) and lists (100 checks, 250 vs 5000) still cover list-length cost. The remaining gap is a term that is quadratic only in the product (calls x list length), such as a per-call cache keyed on the full list that grows. It would show up in the call-count ratio at 250 anyway, scaled down. The 10k-call-at-5000 case is no longer run. Acceptable.
- INFO - O(n^2) proofs: 154-158x (parse), 164x (calls), 384-387x (lists), each failing 2 of 2 times. The variants (indexOf de-dup, growing `seen` includes, per-check filter/indexOf de-dup) are genuine quadratics, and the ratios are consistent with the theoretical 20x to 400x range. Credible. The calls proof is weaker than the lists one in absolute terms (164x vs 400x), but is still 5x over the bound.
- LOW test:354 - The 300 s timeout is now very generous (loaded max 19 s, win32 simulation 30 s at loadavg 29). It is harmless, and could be tightened to about 120 s, but the ratio is the guard. Not required.
- INFO test:303-306 - `TM3_PERF_LOG` is opt-in and silent by default. Any non-empty value enables it, including "0"; that is cosmetic.
- INFO - Acceptance: alone p50 4.46 s, max 4.65 s (target < 8 s). The 20 loaded runs gave 0 failures. The report notes the host was loaded by another agent, so the alone numbers are pessimistic. The full suite and tsc are reported clean.
