## Findings
- LOW agent-runner/src/notify.ts:28 - `res.body?.cancel()` is called after `res.text()` has locked the stream. With a real Response, `cancel()` on a locked stream rejects, which is swallowed, so it is effectively a no-op. The real cut-off comes from the aborted signal erroring the in-flight body read in undici. Behaviour is correct, but the explicit cancel is dead code in practice and the test only proves it with a mock. Consider a comment, or `res.body?.cancel()` only when `bodyUsed` is false.
- LOW agent-runner/src/notify.ts:23-24 - the abort listener is never removed when `text()` settles first. Harmless (`once`, signal is short-lived and GC'd), but tidy cleanup would be `removeEventListener`.
- INFO agent-runner/src/notify.ts:76 - the drain shares the request's 10 s budget, so the body gets only what remains of it. Matches the brief ("existing 10 s timeout").
- INFO agent-runner/test/tm2_18_notify_drain.test.ts:61-77 - the timeout test fakes the timeout with a 50 ms signal and a mock body; no real stream is exercised. Acceptable for a unit test.
- INFO coord/reports/TM2-18.md - outside the brief's allowed list; normal coordination file. LOW follow-ups from TM2-6 are accounted for (applied: drain and destructured fetch; already done: error cap, surrogate; skipped with reason: items in other files).
